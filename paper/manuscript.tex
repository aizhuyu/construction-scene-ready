\title{\systemname: Multi-scale IFC-to-OpenUSD compilation and
scene-level validation for humanoid robotic assembly}

% Author identities are intentionally kept in title-page.tex.

\begin{abstract}
Robot-ready construction simulation requires more than converting building
geometry into a simulation format. A composed scene must preserve engineering
identity, expose assembly interfaces and construction states, allocate physical
fidelity at the scale required by the active task, and close all references
among the building, robot, tool, and task. Existing simulation-readiness
specifications primarily validate individual assets, leaving scene-level
construction task readiness to expensive runtime discovery. This study proposes
\systemname, a multi-scale IFC-to-OpenUSD compiler and scene-level validation
framework for humanoid robotic assembly. It represents construction-task
readiness as an explicit contract and compiles the IFC source into
global-building, active-work-zone, and interaction-level USD layers. Critical
physical properties retain their provenance and uncertainty. When
deterministic validation fails, a language-model agent may diagnose the
structured report and invoke only approved engineering repair tools. A
fault-injection benchmark and paired humanoid assembly simulations compare the
framework with direct conversion, fixed-rule conversion, and unconstrained
agentic repair. On a frozen 105-case fault-injection partition the deterministic validator
recovers all 141 expected findings without false positives and repairs 97.1\%
of cases to pristine-scene equivalence, escalating only cases whose repair
would require unsupported physical values; a validator-grounded agent matches
this ceiling (96.8\% valid, zero incorrect repairs) while an unconstrained
agent repairs only 32.8\% and returns validator-passing but incorrect scenes
in 22.7\% of 525 episodes. In 5{,}600 paired humanoid insertion episodes,
symbolic and referential scene defects were discretely fatal, assembly
interface clearance was the most sensitive continuous feature, and absolute
member-pose errors were absorbed by closed-loop perception. The framework is intended to make scene-model failures observable
before they become costly robot-runtime failures.
\end{abstract}
