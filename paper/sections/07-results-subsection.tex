\subsection{Humanoid task outcomes under scene-model feature defects}
\label{sec:task-outcomes}

The runtime executor for the defect-consequence study is a frozen
reinforcement-learning insertion policy on the \texttt{Isaac-Assembly-G1}
task: a Unitree G1 with a locked lower body transports a virtual-grasped M20
bolt and inserts it along the horizontal axis into the lower-right hole of a
$2\times2$ bolt group on the beam end plate. Success follows the same
criterion as the training reward: transverse alignment error below 12\,mm and
insertion depth of at least 20\,mm from the plate surface. The checkpoint used
(model\_10249) achieves 39.0\% success over 200 evaluation episodes
(38.1\% over 1000 episodes) without injected defects, consistent with its
previously measured 35.5\% baseline under an unfixed seed.

Defects from the six feature families of \Cref{sec:experiments} were injected
at episode reset as controlled deviations between the scene model observed by
the policy and the ground-truth insertion target, at graded severities. All
conditions share one frozen policy, one seed partition, and an identical
episode-randomization stream: the injection events consume the same amount of
randomness whether or not a defect is active, so episode $k$ under any defect
condition is the exact paired replicate of episode $k$ under the defect-free
baseline. Paired binary outcomes are compared with exact two-sided McNemar
tests; $b$/$c$ denote episodes that changed from success to failure and from
failure to success, respectively.

\Cref{tab:defect-decay} yields a clear criticality ordering. Symbolic and
referential features---task references, construction states, and semantic
identity---are discretely fatal: a single wrong reference drives success to
zero because the executor's target is defined by them. Among continuous
features, the assembly interface is the most sensitive: shrinking the
effective radial clearance of the nominal \O22\,mm hole from 1\,mm to
0.75\,mm already collapses success from 39\% to 5\%, and the diagnostic
per-episode record shows the clearance gate truncating mean insertion travel
from $\approx$50\,mm to below 5\,mm, reproducing the physical jamming
mechanism rather than an artefact of the success metric.

The geometric family separates two effects that are easily conflated. A 10\,mm
error in the \emph{absolute} pose of the whole connection node is almost
entirely compensated by the policy's closed-loop perception
($-$2.5\,pp, $b$/$c$=16/11, n.s.), which was trained with $\pm$5\,cm domain
randomization of the node pose. The same 10\,mm applied to the hole position
\emph{relative to the observed member}---the quantity the scene model actually
certifies and the policy cannot see---reduces success significantly
($-$5.5\,pp, $p<0.001$). Repeating the geometric conditions with 1000 paired
episodes sharpens this gradient: 2\,mm remains non-significant
(37.5\% vs.\ 38.1\% baseline, $p=0.109$), 5\,mm becomes significant
(36.8\%, $p=0.015$), and 10\,mm is strongly significant (31.3\%,
$p=5.4\times10^{-16}$), while the whole-member shift remains non-significant
(35.8\%, $p=0.064$). What matters for task success is therefore not the
accuracy of absolute coordinates but the accuracy of coordinates \emph{relative
to the perceptual anchor} used by the controller.

Physical-property defects (bolt mass $\times0.5$ and $\times2$) produced
bit-identical outcomes ($b$/$c$=0/0). This null result is structural, not
statistical: the executor's virtual-grasp scaffold makes the bolt kinematic,
so mass and friction have no dynamical channel in this task stage. We
therefore report the physical family as \emph{not consequential for the
transport-and-insert motion chain} and defer its force-level criticality to a
full-contact ablation (\Cref{sec:discussion}).

All findings replicate under a second seed partition (seed 123, 200 paired
episodes per condition): success rates deviate by at most 2.5\,pp from the
seed-42 values, and every condition that was significant remains significant
with the same direction and comparable magnitude, while every non-significant
condition remains non-significant (\Cref{sec:discussion}). Under seed 123 the
5\,mm hole-position defect is significant already at 200 episodes
(35.0\% vs.\ 40.0\% baseline, $p=0.002$), corroborating the power-upgraded
seed-42 gradient.
