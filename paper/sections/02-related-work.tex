\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Construction robotics and building information}

Construction robotics has long been framed as an information problem as much
as a machine problem: surveys repeatedly identify localization, work
preparation, interoperability, and adaptation to unstructured conditions as
persistent barriers
\cite{vaha2013automation,bock2015future,ardiny2015review,davila2019challenges,melenbrink2020onsite,xiao2022advancements}.
Because collective fabrication, on-site mobile systems, prefabrication, and
human--robot collaboration impose different runtime requirements
\cite{petersen2019collective,xu2026review,afaq2026steel}, ``robot-ready''
cannot denote a single geometric level of detail or one fixed attribute set.
Reviews of BIM--robot interaction likewise document a progression from
one-way export towards closed-loop exchange
\cite{zhang2022bimfar,anane2023interoperability,yue2025interactions,odugu2025review},
in which the receiving robotic system still creates its own reduced
representation---a navigation map, an assembly graph, an SDF world, or an
articulated physics asset---each making different claims about what
information is complete and valid.

Several systems convert building objects into executable task information:
BIM geometry and design context for recognition, grasp search, and motion
planning \cite{dawod2019recognition,ding2020brick}; automated generation of
robot simulation input for timber frames \cite{wong2022wood}; BIM-based task
and motion planning for lightweight structures \cite{gao2022assembly}; an
SDF world configured from BIM for robot painting \cite{kim2021bimrobot}; an
open IFC-to-SDF pipeline preserving element identity for light-steel
assembly \cite{zhu2023ifcsdf}; and action-level links from IFC to ordered
construction actions and ROS execution \cite{zhu2024rcan,oyediran2025fourdbim}.
None of these defines an acceptance test for the composed simulator stage:
whether all assets, references, intermediate states, and physical
assumptions form a valid task scene is left implicit. Semantic world
modelling provides a complementary route---building-element semantics,
maps, waypoints, and schedule-aware environments
\cite{kim2022semantic,moura2021localization,gopee2023navigation,bahreini2024ontology,amani2025pathfinding,chen2023inspection,zhao2024progress}---but
its unit of analysis is a map or knowledge base, whereas assembly readiness
additionally requires valid contact interfaces, physical parameters, object
states, and task success conditions.

\subsection{Physics-ready assets and multi-scale scene composition}

Robotic simulation depends on asset properties that BIM models rarely
provide in executable form. Xu et al.\ fused CAD geometry, equipment
templates, and specifications into articulated USD models of a wheel loader
\cite{xu2024physics}---a close precedent for automated physics-ready
generation, but scoped to equipment modelling rather than to composing
building components, temporary states, task interfaces, and robot
capabilities into one scene. General-purpose simulators supply efficient
contact dynamics and GPU-parallel worlds
\cite{todorov2012mujoco,makoviychuk2021isaacgym}, and domain randomization
exposes policies to parameter variation \cite{tobin2017domain}; none of
these establishes that a source model was compiled correctly. OpenUSD's
layers, references, variants, and payloads separate asset definitions from
scene opinions and permit on-demand loading \cite{openUSD}, which suits
construction: a building shell may support coarse navigation while an active
connection requires detailed collision geometry. Ren et al.'s
specification-driven IFC-to-USD pipeline \cite{ren2025ifcusd} already
provides lifecycle-oriented semantic preservation and configurable delivery;
neither is claimed as new here. What remains open is how a current task
activates geometric scales, interfaces, temporary states, and cross-asset
acceptance conditions on top of IfcOpenShell's IFC4.3 access
\cite{ifc43,ifcopenshellDocs}.

SimReady adds machine-readable asset capabilities and validation profiles
\cite{nvidiaSimReadyWorkflow}, but its official FAQ identifies scene-level
specification and validation as a current limitation
\cite{nvidiaSimReadyFAQ}; \systemname{} builds on asset-level checks by adding
relations that become meaningful only after a task scene is composed.
Embodied scene generation---SimRecon, EmbodiedGen V2, Agentic Real2Sim
\cite{xia2026simrecon,wang2026embodiedgenv2,chen2026agenticreal2sim}---shows
that runnable, physically enriched scenes can be produced by perception or
generation, but their source of authority is visual observation or a world
specification, whereas a construction scene must also remain accountable to
IFC identity, engineering interfaces, process state, and bounded parameter
sources. Finally, using a humanoid evaluator makes the cross-scale
requirement visible: whole-body outcomes depend on coupling among support,
reach, contact, and object dynamics \cite{sferrazza2024humanoidbench}, and
BIM-to-humanoid simulation itself is not new \cite{ye2026humanoiddt}. The
present G1 platform is therefore an evaluation stress test, not a standalone
contribution.

\subsection{Closed-loop construction twins and LLM-assisted engineering}

Digital-twin research addresses the discrepancy between design models and
changing sites, connecting heterogeneous representations and closing the
loop from observation to control
\cite{boje2020semantic,sacks2020digitaltwin,wang2024closeddt,tuomisto2026inspection,akhavian2026bim2rdt}.
These approaches validate the world through runtime sensing and interaction;
the present work addresses the complementary stage of compiling an auditable
physical scene \emph{before} simulation. Engineering analysis can also act
as an acceptance gate: temporary structural stability can modify a robot
assembly plan \cite{xu2025fem}, and semantic-web enrichment can supplement
an observed component with design requirements \cite{zhu2025perception}.
Readiness is thus conditional on the current state and action, not an
intrinsic asset label.

Language models have begun to support construction-robot planning and
engineering-model generation
\cite{you2023robogpt,xu2024physics,akhavian2026bim2rdt}, and the wider
robotics literature grounds language in executable behaviour through
affordance values, environment feedback, program structure, and multimodal
policies
\cite{ahn2022saycan,huang2022innermonologue,liang2023code,singh2023progprompt,driess2023palme,brohan2023rt2,kannan2023smartllm}.
None of these makes a language model an authoritative source for an
engineering mass, tolerance, coordinate frame, or IFC identity. In
\systemname{}, the agent is therefore not a validator or a source of ground
truth: it receives a typed error report, chooses from bounded repair tools,
records evidence for each mutation, and returns the scene to deterministic
rules. This separation of proposal from acceptance is consistent with hybrid
planning \cite{lin2025asp} and with fault-oriented robot assurance
\cite{frasheri2023safer}, and it makes fixed-rule and agentic methods
comparable on identical injected faults.

\subsection{Synthesis of the research gap}

\Cref{tab:literature} compares the closest methodological precedents. The
reviewed literature establishes IFC-to-simulator conversion, BIM-derived
robot tasks, semantic maps, specification-driven IFC-to-USD delivery,
physics-ready USD asset generation, agentic scene generation,
BIM-to-humanoid simulation, closed-loop twin updating, and
language-model-assisted planning---necessary capabilities that operate on
different units of analysis. We did not identify a study that jointly
evaluates a construction-task scene contract, task-activated multi-scale USD
composition, physical-property provenance, deterministic scene-level fault
detection, validator-grounded repair, and the downstream outcome of a
humanoid assembly task. The unresolved transition is from individually valid
digital assets to an auditable, composed, task-specific construction world.

Two buildingSMART instruments deserve explicit positioning. The Information
Delivery Specification (IDS)~\cite{bsi_ids} provides machine-checkable
information requirements for IFC deliveries, including a \texttt{PartOf}
facet constraining how an object belongs to aggregations, groups, spatial
containers, and nests, and the buildingSMART Data Dictionary
(bSDD)~\cite{bsi_bsdd} standardizes the vocabulary those requirements
reference. The present contract does not introduce relation checking for the
first time; its distinct subject is the \emph{robotic task scene}: task
targets resolving to the component owning the bound interface, active
work-zone payloads staying inside the scene package, construction states
gating the next action, and critical physical values carrying authoritative
provenance. These cross-asset binding, composition-safety, process-state,
and evidence-authority requirements are outside what an IFC delivery---even
an IDS-conformant one---is asked to certify. \Cref{sec:results} further
shows empirically that even a fully satisfied contract leaves sub-tolerance
geometric fidelity invisible, which motivates the candidate rules registered
there.
